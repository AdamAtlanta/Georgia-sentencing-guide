## Introductory copy above the embedded tool

Georgia sentencing law can turn on the exact charge, offense degree, drug weight, prior convictions, and other case-specific facts. Use Swingle Levin's interactive guide to look up common Georgia offenses and review the statutory minimum and maximum confinement, fine range, mandatory or must-serve provisions, general parole guidance, and—for covered driving offenses—license consequences.

Start typing an offense or O.C.G.A. section, select a result, and answer any sentencing-factor questions the guide displays. This tool is an educational starting point, not legal advice or a prediction of an actual sentence. Statutes, appellate decisions, and administrative rules change, and charging and sentencing decisions depend on the facts of each case. Each result identifies its sources and legal-review date. If you are facing a Georgia criminal charge, speak with a qualified criminal-defense lawyer about your specific situation.

## Understanding a possible sentence is only one part of a criminal case

The sentence authorized by a statute may differ from the outcome in a particular case. Alleged conduct, prior convictions, mandatory-minimum provisions, merger, probation eligibility, parole rules, local practice, and negotiated resolutions can all matter. Laws and court decisions can also change after this guide's most recent review.

If you or someone you care about is facing a criminal charge in Georgia, speak with an attorney about the actual accusation and available defenses before relying on a general reference.
